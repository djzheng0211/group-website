## Motivation

Biological systems have spent billions of years optimizing catalysis and ion
transport. Enzymes achieve extraordinary rate accelerations and selectivity
under mild, aqueous conditions, and ion channels and pumps move specific ions
across membranes with a speed and selectivity synthetic materials rarely
match. Decades of structural and mechanistic data on these systems already
exist — the opportunity is to mine it systematically rather than draw on it
case by case, and use what it reveals to guide synthetic design.

## Approach

We build datasets of structure–function relationships in biological
catalysts and ion-transport proteins, then use data-driven models to
translate the underlying design principles — active-site architecture,
chirality, selective binding — into synthetic materials.

- **Enzyme-inspired catalysts:** mimicking active-site geometry and local
  environment to improve activity and selectivity
- **Channel-inspired membranes:** borrowing selectivity mechanisms from
  biological ion channels to design synthetic membranes with tailored ion
  selectivity
- **Structure–function mapping:** curating biological and synthetic
  structure–function data side by side and using machine learning to
  identify design rules that transfer
- **Model-guided design:** using those learned rules to propose and
  prioritize new synthetic candidates for the autonomous discovery platform
