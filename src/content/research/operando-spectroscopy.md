## Motivation

Catalysts can transform during operation, while their reactivity also depends
on solvent structure, local pH, ion concentrations, and interfacial electric
fields. Understanding these coupled effects is essential for connecting
structure to function and developing reliable design principles. We combine
operando measurements with machine learning to track the working catalyst and
its microenvironment, extracting mechanistic insight from complex spectral
and temporal data.

## Approach

We combine spectroscopic and isotopic methods to observe the working
interface — and what surrounds it — directly, then use data-driven models to
turn those measurements into structure and mechanism.

- **Spectroscopy:** operando measurements through the reaction, not before
  and after it, to track the active phase as it forms
- **Microenvironment probes:** interfacial solvent structure, local pH, ion
  distribution, and electric field, measured alongside the catalyst itself
- **Isotope labeling:** tracing where lattice oxygen goes to distinguish
  competing mechanisms
- **Mass spectrometry:** product detection resolved in time and potential
- **Machine learning:** models trained on spectral, isotopic, and
  mass-spectrometry data together to extract active-phase and
  microenvironment descriptors in real time, and to flag mechanistic
  changes as conditions shift
